Grok Bot for Recruiting

Sep 24, 2026

How our recruiting team uses Grok Bot (xAI's AI assistant that comes with its own computer) for interview prep, scorecards, sourcing, and loop analysis across the ATS.

Download for macOSContact sales

Grok Bot has dramatically changed the way our Recruiting team works.

Picture this: you're driving to work and realize you need to make a change in your ATS, submit a scorecard, ask a hiring manager to review a candidate, or a dozen other timely projects that pop up. You open Grok Bot, switch to voice mode, and say what you need. It feels like calling a coworker who happens to be sitting at your desk.

That same morning, Grok Bot reviewed a stack of interview transcripts against our rubric, updated a candidate record, and drafted a scorecard before I got to my desk. The point isn't that it can multitask. It's that Grok Bot sits across our recruiting tools, so the scorecard, the hiring-manager ask, and the transcripts aren't three systems you have to open yourself. If you can do it in the stack, Grok Bot can do it too. This gives our recruiting team more time to focus on candidates and deliver our best work.

Fast adoption

When we started rolling this out, adoption spread fast. Within a week we had pages of use cases, with recruiters sharing prompts and routines with each other.

We ran a recruiting offsite where we split into small teams and each built a bot aimed at a day-to-day recruiting problem. The team built bots that focused on efficiency plays, digging into our own data, and even some humor. Here's what we built, and how we use it for recruiting.

Recruiting bots

Preppy

Interviewers are slammed, and candidate context lives in a million places: Notion (a notes and database app), LinkedIn, the ATS. Right before the call, Preppy drops one Slack (a work chat app) note that consolidates who you're talking to, why we're excited about them, interview stage, and what the focus areas should be (plus what's already been covered). Now all candidate and process context is in one spot instead of a scavenger hunt.

Add Preppy to Grok Bot

Pulse

After a long day of recruiter screens, candidates blend together. After an interview, Pulse pulls the Granola transcript or meeting recording, matches it to the scorecard for that role, and drafts the interview feedback form in your voice. Then it pauses and asks you a few follow-ups for your take (rating, how it felt, anything fuzzy, what you'd pressure-test next) so the form isn't just a transcript dump. You answer the follow-ups, it redrafts and submits to your ATS. Scorecards stop being the thing you put off until 9pm.

Add Pulse to Grok Bot

Scout

This bot reads scorecards and resumes across hundreds of hired and rejected candidates, so it learns what worked and what didn't. It also sources candidates overnight, adds them to the ATS as leads, and drafts multi-touch sequences so a shortlist is ready when you open your laptop. You approve the send and then Scout moves them to Reached Out and watches for replies.

Add Scout to Grok Bot

eVal

Looks across the loop for where candidates are failing and why, then turns this data into questions you can start asking on the recruiter screen. Flags get caught earlier instead of burning interviewer time downstream.

Add eVal to Grok Bot

Getting the most out of Grok Bot

  • Onboard Grok Bot like a new teammate. The first time you do a task together (like writing an outreach note), walk it through your reasoning, then have it turn that into a reusable skill.
  • Feed it your voice. Have it read your best messaging, debrief notes, and offer emails before it drafts anything new. You don't need to give it all this information. Just tell your bot where to find it in your systems, the way you would a teammate.
  • Turn corrections into standing rules. If your bot keeps writing outreach that's too salesy, or debrief notes that are too vague, give it direct feedback and your bot will remember.
  • Let it parallelize. A sourcing agent and a feedback agent can run at once, across different reqs, instead of one at a time.
  • Chain agents together so you're not the glue. eVal sees where people fail downstream and sharpens the ICP. Scout hunts from that, adds leads, and drafts outreach you approve. Preppy briefs the interviewer. Pulse drafts the scorecard and asks for your take. Context moves with the candidate between your bots. You're not copying notes across Notion, Slack, and Ashby, or explaining the same person four times.

None of this replaces the recruiter. It clears out everything standing between you, the candidate, and your stakeholders. Most of my day is candidate-facing again: interviews, debriefs, and calibration, not scorecards and pipeline reports at 9pm. Faster decisions, better signal, and a fleet that gets sharper the more we use it.
